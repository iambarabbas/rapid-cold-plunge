The Science

What is cold plunge therapy?

Cold water immersion — or cold plunge therapy — is the practice of submerging the body in cold water for a controlled period of time. It's been used for centuries, and modern science has caught up to explain exactly why it works so well.

When you enter cold water, your blood vessels rapidly constrict (vasoconstriction), redirecting warm blood to protect your core organs. As you exit, they dilate again — flushing out metabolic waste from muscles and delivering a surge of oxygenated blood. This mechanism is a key driver of faster muscle recovery.

The cold also triggers a significant dopamine release — up to 250% above baseline, sustained for hours. Combined with a norepinephrine spike that sharpens attention and mood, the post-plunge state is one of the most naturally productive you can achieve. No caffeine required.

Benefits
❄️ Faster muscle recovery Vasoconstriction followed by vasodilation flushes lactate and reduces inflammation, helping athletes recover up to 24 hours faster.
🧠 Sharpened focus A sustained dopamine and norepinephrine release creates a state of calm clarity that persists for hours after each session.
🛡️ Strengthened immune response Regular cold exposure increases circulating white blood cell count and activates innate immune defenses over time.
💤 Improved sleep Cold immersion lowers core body temperature, a critical trigger for sleep onset. Many members report deeper, more consistent sleep.
⚡ Natural energy without caffeine The neurochemical cascade from cold exposure delivers real, sustained energy — no crash, no dependency.

The Protocol

Simple protocol.

Here's what a cold plunge session looks like at RAPID. Straightforward. No complexity.

01

Arrive & check in

Walk in, check in with our guide. They'll answer any questions and brief first-timers on what to expect.

02

Quick shower

A brief rinse before you enter the plunge. Standard protocol, keeps everything clean.

03

Enter the plunge

Step in, submerge to your shoulders. Your guide sets a timer. Stay calm — the first 20 seconds are the hardest.

04

Breathe through it

Slow, controlled exhales. Focus on your breath. The urge to gasp and exit passes quickly — what follows is remarkable.

05

Exit, recover, feel it

Step out. Let your body warm naturally. Take a few minutes in the recovery area and notice how you feel. That's the protocol working.
